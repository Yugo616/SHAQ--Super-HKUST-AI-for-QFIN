# Foundations

- Cieslak and Pang, "Common Shocks in Stocks and Bonds," Journal of Financial Economics 2021. Four shocks (growth, monetary, common premium and hedging premium) are identified from daily stock returns and Treasury-yield changes by their joint sign and their maturity profile: growth and monetary shocks weaken with maturity while premium shocks strengthen with it, so each stock-yield sign quadrant holds one short-rate and one premium candidate. A rising hedging premium lowers stocks while bonds rally (yields fall), the flight-to-safety case; a rising common premium lowers both (yields rise), reflecting shared exposure to discount-rate risk: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.06.008>
- Campbell and Ammer, "What Moves the Stock and Bond Markets? A Variance Decomposition for Long-Term Asset Returns," Journal of Finance 1993. Unexpected stock-index returns decompose into news about future cash flows, real interest rates and future excess returns (the risk premium), with risk-premium news the largest part in postwar monthly data: <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04700.x>
- Barclay and Hendershott, "Price Discovery and Trading After Hours," Review of Financial Studies 2003. For the 250 most traded Nasdaq stocks in 2000, preopen prices carried a signal-to-noise ratio of about 0.8 to 0.9 against about 1 during the trading day, price discovery was more complete for more heavily traded stocks, and more than half of close-to-open price discovery arrived in the last half hour before the open: <https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/16/4/1041/1576361>
- Lou, Polk and Skouras, "A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected Returns," Journal of Financial Economics 2019. Overnight and intraday returns reflect different clienteles; for individual stocks and trading strategies the two components persist separately and pull in opposite directions over months, while for the market index both are positive on average: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.03.011>
- Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, "Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum," Journal of Financial Economics 2021. Across 62 futures from 1974 to 2020, the return from the previous close to 30 minutes before the close predicts the final 30 minutes; once that return is known, the overnight-plus-first-half-hour return adds nothing, and for S&P 500 futures the effect appears only when option dealers are net short gamma. It is not conditioned on any macro mechanism: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.04.029>
- Frazzini and Pedersen, "Betting Against Beta," Journal of Financial Economics 2014. A funding shock pushes all security prices down together, so when funding-liquidity risk rises realized betas compress toward one; the cross-sectional dispersion of US equity betas falls from 0.34 to 0.29 between low and high TED-spread-volatility months: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.10.005>
- Adolfsen, Grønlund and Harr, "The US Dollar: Not a Traditional Safe Haven," VoxEU/CEPR 2026. A safe-haven shock, measured by a factor that includes the VIX, lifts the dollar only for a few days and weakens it against the yen and Swiss franc; only a funding-stress shock, measured from interbank funding spreads and covered-interest-parity deviations, strengthens it persistently and broadly, and on impact the two look alike: <https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/us-dollar-not-traditional-safe-haven>
- Arnal and Pinto, "Exorbitant Privilege under Stress? Evidence of Episodic Weakening in the Dollar's Safe-Haven Behaviour," Cogent Economics & Finance 2026. In weekly data from 2000 to 2025, 10-year Treasury yields usually fall as the VIX rises, including in the financial crisis and COVID; in the early-2025 US policy-credibility episode that relation turned positive in February and March before reverting, and the dollar's co-movement with the VIX turned negative: <https://doi.org/10.1080/23322039.2026.2646789>
- Molenaar, Senechal, Swinkels and Wang, "Empirical Evidence on the Stock-Bond Correlation," Financial Analysts Journal 2024. Since 1951 the US stock-bond correlation has been higher when inflation and real interest rates are higher, and more positive where government bonds are seen as riskier; it averaged +0.35 in 1970-1999 and -0.29 in 2000-2023: <https://doi.org/10.1080/0015198X.2024.2317333>

System rule: classify the cross-asset quadrant, its breadth across size and style, and how much is already absorbed, each separately. Never map an overnight move directly to the open-to-close direction: the premarket leg is a noisy, partial reading, and the documented intraday momentum runs from the day's cumulative move into its last half hour, only when dealers are short gamma, and is not tied to any mechanism. Each quadrant holds two candidate shocks that the benchmark set cannot separate. Down-weight the beta channel toward neutral when the exposure is unstable across estimation windows or swamped by residual volatility. Treat a stated beta far from one as overstating the difference from the market when funding liquidity tightens, which the inputs cannot detect: a caution on magnitude, never a reason to flip the sign. Which reading of a joint equity-Treasury move counts as normal depends on the prevailing inflation and real-rate regime.
